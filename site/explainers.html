Websites and marketing

1 explainer
Explainer No. 1 · Websites

Our cookie banner has no “reject” button. How big are the fines?

Potentially very big. Refusing must be as easy as accepting.

€150mGoogle
€60mFacebook

Fined by France’s regulator (CNIL) in Dec 2021: accepting took one click, refusing took several.

  • ePrivacy Art. 5(3)
  • GDPR Art. 7(3)
  • Recital 42

1 explainer
Explainer No. 2 · Staff

We want to monitor staff laptops. Has a regulator ruled on this?

Yes, many times. Limited, announced monitoring can be lawful. Constant or hidden monitoring gets fined.

€35.3mH&M, Germany

Fined in 2020 for notes on staff’s private lives. Others: screen recording, reading email, tracking apps.

  • GDPR Art. 5(1)(c)
  • Art. 6(1)(f)
  • Art. 88
Finofinoprivacy.com

Sources: Hamburg regulator, Oct 2020, via CMS; other decisions via GDPRhub. Checked 6 Oct 2026.

Security and breaches

1 explainer
Explainer No. 3 · Security

The board wants to know our worst-case fine for this data breach.

Up to €20 million or 4% of group turnover, whichever is higher.

€251mMeta, 2024
£20mBritish Airways

Real fines are usually far lower: British Airways was first told £183m, and paid £20m.

  • GDPR Art. 32
  • Art. 33
  • Art. 83
Finofinoprivacy.com

Sources: Irish regulator, Dec 2024; UK regulator, Oct 2020; via CMS. Checked 6 Oct 2026.

AI and new tech

1 explainer
Explainer No. 4 · AI

We’re training AI on customer data. Has anyone been fined?

Yes. Training is usually a new purpose: justify it, tell customers, let them object.

€15mOpenAI, Italy
€30.5mClearview AI

Italy fined OpenAI (Dec 2024) for training ChatGPT without a proper legal basis.

  • GDPR Art. 5(1)(b)
  • Art. 6(4)
  • Art. 21
Finofinoprivacy.com

Sources: Italian and Dutch regulators, 2024, via GDPRhub and CMS. Checked 6 Oct 2026.
